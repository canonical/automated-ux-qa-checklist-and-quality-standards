## Issues by page section

### Hero

**[Accessibility] 🔵 Minor – Generic iframe title on YouTube embed**
Evidence: `title="YouTube video player"`
The iframe title is the label screen readers announce when navigating by landmarks. "YouTube video player" gives no context about what this specific video covers. Replace with a title that describes the video content, e.g., "How Ubuntu Pro supports NIS2 compliance – Canonical".

**[Brand & style – B6] 🔴 Critical – Absolute supply chain security claim**
Evidence: "Take advantage of a complete ecosystem of open source tools and applications, ensuring supply chain security for all of your open source software."
"Ensuring supply chain security for all of your open source software" is an unprovable absolute claim. The Canonical style guide explicitly flags this language. Suggested fix: "…supporting supply chain security for your open source software" or "…helping secure your open source supply chain".

### How Ubuntu Pro enables NIS2 – Supply chain security

**[Brand & style – A13] 🟡 Needs Work – "allows us to" (banned word)**
Evidence: "This allows us to prevent malicious dependencies and upstream backdoors impacting your infrastructure."
"Allow" implies granting permission and is in the Canonical banned words list. Suggested fix: "This helps prevent malicious dependencies and upstream backdoors from impacting your infrastructure."

### How Ubuntu Pro enables NIS2 – Monitoring at scale

Suggested fix: "Landscape is a fleet management tool that lets you manage your fleet through a single pane of glass and keep it updated."

### Security compliance in action

**[Accessibility] 🟡 Needs Work – Case study subheadings are `<p>` elements, not semantic headings**
Evidence: `<p class="p-heading--5">Ubuntu Pro helps Lucid Software meet FedRAMP compliance for government contracts</p>` (same pattern in all three tabs – Lucid, Grundium, OEDIV).
These subheadings are styled with a heading utility class but are marked up as paragraphs. Screen readers will not surface them in the heading outline, which breaks the navigation experience for keyboard and assistive technology users. Use the appropriate heading level (`<h3>` or `<h4>`) with the `.p-heading--5` styling class applied.

**[Links] 🟡 Needs Work – Identical link text goes to three different destinations**
Evidence: "Read the case study ›" appears in each of the three tabs (Lucid Software, Grundium, OEDIV), each linking to a different URL.
This breaks the checklist rule: links with the same text on the same page should go to the same destination. Although tabs hide two of the three links at any given time, assistive technology may list all links regardless of tab state. Suggested fix: make each link specific, e.g., "Read the Lucid Software case study ›", "Read the Grundium case study ›", "Read the OEDIV case study ›".

**[Brand & style – A10] 🔵 Minor – FedRAMP acronym not spelled out on first use**
Evidence: "Ubuntu Pro helps Lucid Software meet FedRAMP compliance for government contracts" (first use on the page).
Per the Canonical style guide, acronyms should be spelled out on first mention with the abbreviation in brackets. Suggested fix: "Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program (FedRAMP) compliance". Given this is a case study heading rather than body copy, this is low priority.

**[Brand & style – A7] 🔵 Minor – Single-digit numbers written as digits (OEDIV tab)**
Evidence: "OEDIV reduces upgrade time by 60% and unlocks 6 years of zero downtime with Ubuntu Pro" and "OEDIV shrank critical maintenance windows from 3 hours down to 45 minutes".
The style guide says spell out single-figure numbers one–nine. Suggested fix: "six years of zero downtime" and "three hours down to 45 minutes".

### What is NIS2?

**[Structure] 🟡 Needs Work – Opening sentence is ~51 words, well above the 20-word guideline**
Evidence: "The NIS2 Directive (Network and Information Security Directive 2) is a European Union cybersecurity regulation that applies to all EU Member States which aims to strengthen cybersecurity by harmonizing security requirements, improving cooperation among countries, and introducing new focus areas such as supply chain security."
This is a single sentence of ~51 words. The style guide targets 12–20 words on average. Suggested fix: split into two sentences – "The NIS2 Directive (Network and Information Security Directive 2) is a European Union cybersecurity regulation. It applies to all EU Member States and aims to strengthen cybersecurity by harmonizing security requirements, improving cooperation among countries, and introducing new focus areas such as supply chain security."

**[Brand & style – B5] 🟡 Needs Work – Passive voice opening**
Evidence: "Implemented into national law by 17 October 2024, NIS2 expands the scope of regulated organizations…"
Suggested fix: "As of October 17, 2024, when it became national law across the EU, NIS2 expands the scope…" or "Member states implemented NIS2 into national law on October 17, 2024; it now expands the scope…"

**[Brand & style – A6] 🔵 Minor – Non-US date format**
Evidence: "17 October 2024"
The Canonical style guide specifies US prose format. Suggested fix: "October 17, 2024".

### Ubuntu Pro supports NIS2 compliance everywhere – In the cloud

**[Links] 🔵 Minor – "Learn more" visible text is generic (aria-labels present)**
Evidence: Three cloud provider links (AWS, Azure, Google Cloud Platform) all display "Learn more ›" as visible text, though each has a descriptive `aria-label`.
The accessibility requirement is technically met by the aria-labels. As a minor copy improvement, making the visible link text match the aria-label would remove the discrepancy between what sighted users see and what assistive technology announces.

---

### Ubuntu Pro supports NIS2 compliance everywhere – In air-gapped environments

**[Links] 🔵 Minor – "here" at the end of linked phrase**
Evidence: `All Ubuntu Pro features are also available in air-gapped or offline deployments. <a href="...">Explore our documentation here</a>.`
While "Explore our documentation here" provides more context than "here" alone, ending a linked phrase with "here" is a pattern to avoid. Suggested fix: "Explore our air-gapped deployment documentation" (making the description the link, dropping "here").
